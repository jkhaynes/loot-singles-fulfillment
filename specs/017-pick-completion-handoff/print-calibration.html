Label print calibration

Task T001 for feature 017. Two separate questions, deliberately decoupled — the first one needs no label printer.

Test A — can the browser print a true physical size?

This is the question that matters most, and any printer with plain paper can answer it. If a browser can't hit a real inch on ordinary paper, no amount of label-stock fiddling will fix it — and that's the failure mode our token-based mitigation can't absorb.

  1. In the print dialog set Scale: 100% (or “Actual size” — not “Fit to page” or “Shrink to fit”).
  2. Paper size Letter, margins Default or None.
  3. Print, then measure the ruler and the 1-inch square with a real ruler or tape.

Report back: does the 6-inch ruler measure 6 inches, and is the square 1 × 1 inch? If they're off, say by how much — a consistent percentage tells us it's scaling, an inconsistent one tells us something stranger.

Test B — can the label printer hit 3.5 × 1.125 in?

  1. Select the label printer and its address-label stock in the print dialog.
  2. Scale 100% / Actual size, margins None.
  3. Print one. Check the label fills the stock without clipping, and measure the small 1-inch reference bar along the bottom edge.
  4. Scan the barcode into any text box — it should type F8433182-7B9B3B-BC75E, matching the text printed under the bars.
  5. Point a phone camera at the QR — it should offer a link ending /orders/121.

Report back: the measured width and height, whether anything is clipped, and whether both codes read. If the size is wrong but consistent, that's a token change in label.css and nothing else moves.
